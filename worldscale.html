Worldscale

Worldscale is a published schedule of reference freight rates for tanker voyages. Tanker freight is negotiated as a percentage of that reference, quoted as "WS" points.

How it works

For each pair of loading and discharging ports, Worldscale publishes a flat rate in US dollars per tonne. It is calculated for a standard ship on a round voyage and reissued each year to reflect fuel prices and port costs. The flat rate is called WS 100.

Owners and charterers then negotiate only the percentage. If the flat rate for a route is USD 20 per tonne and the fixture is done at WS 80, freight is USD 16 per tonne. On an 80,000-tonne cargo that is USD 1.28 million.

Why tankers use it

Tanker charters often give the charterer a range of loading and discharging ports, decided after the ship is fixed. One WS number covers every permitted combination, because each has its own flat rate. Both sides can also compare very different routes on one scale.

What moves the WS level

Supply and demand for that size of tanker in that region. Larger ships fix at lower WS numbers than smaller ones on the same route, because their cost per tonne is lower.

Where it is not used

Dry bulk freight is quoted directly in dollars per tonne. Some tanker fixtures, especially on short or unusual routes, are done as a lump sum. Time charters are quoted in dollars per day. To compare a WS fixture with a daily rate, convert it with a voyage estimate.
