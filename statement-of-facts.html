Statement of facts

A statement of facts is the timed record of everything that happened during a ship’s port call. The laytime and demurrage calculation is built from it.

Who prepares it

Normally the port agent, from the ship’s log and the terminal’s records. It is signed by the master and the agent, and often by the shipper or receiver as well.

What it should contain

  • Arrival at the port or anchorage, and when notice of readiness was tendered and accepted
  • Time of berthing and of any shifting between berths
  • Start and finish of loading or discharging, with every interruption and its cause
  • Weather stoppages, holidays and shifts not worked
  • Draught surveys, hold or tank inspections, and the quantity loaded or discharged
  • Completion, documents on board and sailing

Why to read it before signing

Whether a stoppage counts against laytime depends on its cause and on who was responsible. "Stopped 4 hours" is useless; "stopped 4 hours, rain" or "shore conveyor breakdown" decides who pays. If you disagree with an entry, sign "under protest" and note why.

A shared record avoids the argument

Disputes start when each side has its own version. On FindAVessel, voyage events go into one time log that the owner and charterer each confirm as they happen, so the laytime statement starts from agreed facts.
