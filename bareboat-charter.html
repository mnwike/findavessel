Bareboat charter

Under a bareboat charter, also called a demise charter, the owner hands over the ship with no crew. The charterer mans, maintains, insures and operates it as if it were theirs, usually for several years.

What each side does

The owner provides the vessel and collects hire, typically monthly. The charterer appoints the master and crew, pays for maintenance, dry-docking and insurance, and bears every running cost. The standard form is BIMCO’s BARECON.

Why it is used

  • Finance. A bank or leasing company owns the ship and bareboats it to the operator, often with an option or obligation to buy at the end. It is a lease in all but name.
  • Flag. A bareboat charterer can sometimes register the ship under a second flag for the charter period.
  • Control. An operator gets a ship for years without buying it.

How it differs from a time charter

On a time charter the owner still employs the crew and is responsible for the ship’s condition; the charterer only directs where it goes. On a bareboat charter that responsibility moves to the charterer, who becomes a disponent owner when they charter the ship out again.

If you were looking for yachts

In leisure sailing, "bareboat charter" just means hiring a yacht without a skipper for a holiday. That is a different market, and not what this page or FindAVessel covers.
