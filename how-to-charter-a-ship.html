How to charter a ship

Chartering a ship means hiring the whole vessel, for one voyage or for a period. The process is the same whether the cargo is 5,000 tonnes of steel or two million barrels of crude.

1. Define the enquiry

An owner can only price what is described. A complete cargo order states the cargo and grade, quantity with tolerance, load and discharge ports, the laycan, loading and discharging rates, any limits on the ship such as age or gear, and the charter party form.

2. Put it to the market

Traditionally the enquiry goes to one or more shipbrokers, who pass it to owners. On a platform you post it and choose who may see it. Advertising a cargo widely can push rates up, so sensitive cargoes go to a shortlist.

3. Receive firm offers

An owner's firm offer names the ship, the freight rate, demurrage, laycan and other terms, and is valid for a set time, often a few hours. You accept, decline or counter. Each counter is a new firm offer that replaces the last.

4. Agree main terms "on subjects"

Once the terms match, the deal is agreed subject to listed conditions: management approval, confirmation that the cargo is available (the stem), the shippers' and receivers' approval of the ship, and details of the charter party. Each subject has a deadline.

5. Lift subjects: the ship is fixed

When every subject is lifted the deal is "clean" and binding. The broker issues the recap. From here, walking away is a breach of contract.

6. Perform the voyage

The ship arrives within the laycan and gives notice of readiness. Laytime starts. Cargo is loaded, bills of lading are issued, the ship sails, and the process repeats at the discharge port.

7. Settle

Freight is usually paid within a few banking days of loading, often 95% first with the balance after discharge. Demurrage or despatch is calculated from the statement of facts and settled last.

Unknown counterparties get checked on reputation, finances, sanctions exposure and insurance before a deal becomes binding. Having this ready is the difference between getting an answer and being ignored.

What does it cost?

There is no price list. Freight is set by supply and demand for that size of ship in that region that week. The owner works back from fuel, port costs and what the ship could earn elsewhere. The voyage estimator shows how the sum is built.
