Guides / What is demurrage?

What is demurrage?

Demurrage is money the charterer pays the shipowner when loading or discharging takes longer than the time the contract allows. It compensates the owner for a ship held up in port.

In one line: time used minus time allowed, multiplied by the daily demurrage rate.

How demurrage works in a ship charter

A voyage charter gives the charterer a fixed amount of time to load and discharge, called laytime. The owner has priced the freight on that basis. If the ship is kept longer, the owner loses days it could have spent earning on the next voyage, so the charter party sets a daily rate for the overrun. That rate is demurrage.

The rate is agreed in the negotiation, alongside freight. It usually sits near what the ship could earn per day on the open market.

How to calculate demurrage

  1. Work out the laytime allowed. Either a fixed period, or the cargo quantity divided by the agreed daily rates.
  2. Establish when laytime started, from the notice of readiness and the charter party terms.
  3. Count the time used, leaving out any periods the charter party excludes, such as bad weather or holidays.
  4. Subtract the time allowed from the time used. Multiply any excess by the demurrage rate, pro rata for part days.

Example. 28,000 tonnes loading at 4,000 tonnes a day gives 7 days of laytime. Loading takes 12 days with no excluded time. The ship is on demurrage for 5 days. At USD 12,000 a day the charterer owes USD 60,000. You can run your own figures in the laytime and demurrage calculator.

"Once on demurrage, always on demurrage"

Under most charter parties the exceptions that pause laytime stop applying once laytime has expired. Rain that would not have counted during laytime does count once the ship is on demurrage, unless the contract says otherwise.

Despatch: the reverse case

If the charterer finishes early, many dry cargo charters pay them despatch for the time saved, usually at half the demurrage rate. Tanker charters normally do not.

Charter demurrage and container demurrage are different things

Who pays whomCharterer pays the shipownerImporter or exporter pays the shipping line or terminal

How to avoid disputes

  • Agree the laytime terms precisely: the rates, what counts and what does not.
  • Record events as they happen in a statement of facts both sides sign.
  • Present the claim with supporting documents inside the time bar in the charter party, often 90 days or less.
