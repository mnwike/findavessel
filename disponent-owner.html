Guides / Disponent owner

Disponent owner

A disponent owner is a company that does not own a ship but controls it under a time or bareboat charter, and charters it out to someone else. To the next charterer, they stand in the owner’s place.

How it arises

An operator takes a ship on time charter for a year at a daily rate, then uses it to carry other companies’ cargoes on voyage terms. In each of those voyage charters the operator signs as "disponent owner". Much of the dry bulk market works this way.

What it means if you are the charterer

  • Your contract is with the disponent owner, not the registered owner. Claims for non-performance go to them.
  • Their performance depends on a contract you cannot see. If they stop paying hire, the registered owner can withdraw the ship.
  • Bills of lading are often still the registered owner’s, which can give the cargo owner a direct claim for loss or damage to the goods.

What to check

  • Who the registered owner is, and that the disponent owner really has the ship on charter for long enough to perform your voyage.
  • The disponent owner’s financial standing: they may have few assets of their own.
  • That the ship’s P&I cover and class are in order, exactly as you would for an owner.

On FindAVessel a vessel listing states whether it is posted by the registered owner, a disponent owner or a manager, and the authority behind it is checked before the listing goes live.
