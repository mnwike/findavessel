VLCC: size, capacity and trades

A VLCC, or very large crude carrier, is an oil tanker of roughly 200,000 to 320,000 deadweight tonnes. One cargo is about two million barrels of crude oil.

Key facts

VLCC
Deadweight200,000 to 320,000 tonnes
Typical cargoAbout 2 million barrels of crude oil
Typical length and beamAbout 330 m by 60 m
Laden draughtAbout 20 to 22 m
Next size downSuezmax
Next size upULCC, above 320,000 tonnes

Where VLCCs trade

VLCCs carry crude on the long hauls where scale pays: the Arabian Gulf to China, Japan, Korea and India, West Africa to Asia, and the US Gulf to Asia. The cost per barrel falls as the ship gets bigger, which is why the longest routes use the largest ships.

What limits them

Draught. A laden VLCC sits too deep for most ports, so cargo is loaded and discharged at offshore terminals and single-point moorings, or transferred to smaller tankers at sea. A fully laden VLCC is too deep for the Suez Canal and too wide for the Panama Canal, so routing is part of every freight calculation.

How VLCCs are chartered

Mostly on voyage charters with freight quoted in Worldscale points, on forms such as ASBATANKVOY or SHELLVOY. Charterers are oil majors, national oil companies and large traders, and every ship is vetted before it is accepted. This is the hardest corner of the tanker market for a newcomer to enter.
