Ultramax: size, capacity and trades

An Ultramax is a geared bulk carrier of roughly 60,000 to 67,000 deadweight tonnes: the larger, more fuel-efficient successor to the Supramax.

Key facts

Ultramax
Deadweight60,000 to 67,000 tonnes
Holds and gearFive holds, four cranes of 30 to 36 tonnes, usually with grabs
Typical length and beamAbout 200 m by 32.26 m
Next size downSupramax
Next size upPanamax

Why the class appeared

Shipyards found they could add several thousand tonnes of capacity to a Supramax design for little extra fuel. Charterers get more cargo per voyage and the ship can still work its own cargo with its cranes.

What Ultramaxes carry

The same wide mix as a Supramax: grain, coal, fertiliser, cement and clinker, bauxite, nickel ore, steel and other minor bulks. The extra capacity matters most on long hauls.

Chartering one

Cargoes of 55,000 to 63,000 tonnes are a natural fit. State whether you need the ship’s cranes and grabs at either port, because a geared ship costs more than a gearless one of the same size and you should not pay for gear you will not use.
