What deadweight tonnage means

Deadweight tonnage, or DWT, is the weight a ship can carry when loaded to its summer load line: cargo plus fuel, fresh water, stores and crew. It is not the weight of the ship itself. Tankers and bulk carriers are classed by DWT. Container ships are classed by TEU, the number of twenty-foot containers they hold, and gas carriers by tank volume in cubic metres.

Why the bands are not exact

These names grew out of trade practice. Some come from canals (Panamax, Suezmax), some from ports (Kamsarmax, Newcastlemax), one from a freight scale (Aframax). Brokers, exchanges and shipyards draw the lines in slightly different places. A contract should state the actual deadweight, not just the class name.
