Laytime explained

Laytime is the time a voyage charter allows the charterer to load and discharge the cargo without paying extra. When it runs out, demurrage begins.

When laytime starts

Three things normally have to happen. The ship must reach the place named in the charter party, which may be a berth or the port. It must be ready in every respect to load or discharge. And the master must give notice of readiness to the charterer or its agent. Most contracts then add a short turn time, for example six hours, before the clock starts.

A notice given before the ship is truly ready is invalid, and a great many laytime disputes begin there.

How much time is allowed

Either a fixed period, such as 72 running hours, or a rate: for example 8,000 tonnes per day loading and 4,000 discharging. With rates, laytime is the cargo quantity divided by the rate. Time at the two ports is either counted separately or pooled, depending on whether the charter says "reversible".

What counts and what does not

TermMeaning
SHINCSundays and holidays included. Every day counts.
SHEXSundays and holidays excepted. Those days do not count.
Weather working dayA working day on which weather does not stop cargo work. Time lost to weather is not counted.
Running hoursConsecutive hours with no exceptions, common in tanker charters.
WIBONWhether in berth or not. Notice may be given while waiting for a berth.
ReversibleLoading and discharging time are added together and used as one allowance.

When laytime ends

When cargo work is complete, or when the allowed time is used up, whichever comes first. If time runs out first the ship goes on demurrage. If work finishes first, a dry cargo charterer may earn despatch.

The statement of facts

The port agent records each event with its time: arrival, notice, berthing, start and stop of cargo work, weather stoppages, completion. Both sides rely on this record for the laytime calculation, so read it before signing. On FindAVessel the same events go into a shared time log that both parties confirm as they happen.
